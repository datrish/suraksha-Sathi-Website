The problem

Care cannot end when a mother leaves the hospital.

Between visits, mothers are on their own, and staff have little time to keep track of every one of them.

  • 01
    Contact drops after the visitOnce she leaves, there is often no routine way to check how she and the baby are doing.
  • 02
    Milestones slipCheck-ups, scans and vaccine doses are easy to miss when everything lives on paper and memory.
  • 03
    Many families don't use appsSMS and app reminders don't reach mothers who don't read or don't use a smartphone.
  • 04
    Staff ring round by handReception spends hours on repeat phone calls that could go to mothers who need a person.
  • 05
    Worries go unheardA concern a mother mentions on the phone is easy to lose before it reaches anyone clinical.
  • 06
    No clear viewIt is hard to see who has been contacted, who hasn't, and who needs attention today.
How it works

From enrolment to your team's daily list.

  1. EnrolReception adds the mother in about 30 seconds and records her consent.
  2. CallSuraksha Sathi calls on schedule in Hindi, 10am–7pm, with one retry, then WhatsApp.
  3. RespondThe mother answers. A husband or close family member can take a reminder.
  4. CaptureAnswers are recorded and structured against the questions you approved.
  5. FlagReported warning signs are flagged using your doctor-approved protocol.
  6. ReviewFlags, missed calls and outcomes appear on your list. Your team decides what to do.
Automated by Suraksha SathiDone by your team

Flagging is not clinical review. Suraksha Sathi only follows the protocol you approve and puts concerns in front of your team. It does not diagnose, decide or promise a callback. Clinical judgement stays with you.

Safety and responsible use

Firm rules, set before any real call.

  • Recorded consentConsent is recorded at enrolment. Mothers can stop the calls at any time.
  • Your doctor signs offNothing goes live until your doctor approves the schedule and every call script.
  • Never discusses the baby's sexA fixed rule the AI cannot override (PCPNDT Act).
  • No diagnosis, no medicine adviceIt never suggests delaying a dose and only uses answers you have approved.
  • Concerns go to clinical reviewReported warning signs are flagged to your team, never handled by the AI alone.
  • Not an emergency serviceMothers are told to contact the hospital or dial 108 when they need urgent help.
  • Her health stays with herA family member can take a reminder; questions about the mother's own health are for her.
  • Clear about being AIMothers are told the call is from a computer voice, on behalf of your hospital.

This describes how Suraksha Sathi is designed. It is not legal advice, a certification or a regulatory approval.
